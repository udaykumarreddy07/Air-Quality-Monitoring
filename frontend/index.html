Clinical Vulnerability & Sensitive Population Advisory

Epidemiological risk stratification calibrated against statutory CPCB & WHO guidelines

👶 Children & Infants MODERATE RISK

Developing alveoli and higher ventilation rates per body weight increase fine particle deposition. Safe for outdoor play, but restrict heavy cardio when AQI > 150.

👵 Elderly (65+ Cohort) ELEVATED RISK

Elevated vascular inflammation and ischemic cardiac stress risks from fine particles (PM2.5). Schedule morning walks post-sunrise to avoid ground inversions.

🫁 Asthmatics & COPD HIGH ALERT

Bronchial hyper-responsiveness triggered by NO2 and PM2.5. Keep quick-relief inhalers (bronchodilators) on hand; utilize indoor HEPA air purification.

🏃 Outdoor Workers & Athletes ACTIVE MONITORING

High volume oral respiration bypasses nasal cilia filtration. Limit prolonged outdoor workouts during evening rush hour peaks (19:00 - 22:00); wear certified N95 respirators.

Good (AQI 0 - 50)

Air quality is considered satisfactory, and air pollution poses little or no risk. Ideal for all outdoor exercises and sports.

Moderate (AQI 51 - 100)

Air quality is acceptable. Sensitive individuals with respiratory conditions may experience minor breathing discomfort.

Unhealthy for Sensitive (AQI 101 - 150)

Children, elderly, and people with heart or lung disease should reduce prolonged outdoor exertion and wear N95 masks.

Unhealthy (AQI 151 - 200)

General public begins to experience health effects; sensitive groups experience more serious effects. Avoid outdoor jogs.

Very Unhealthy / Severe (AQI 201+)

Emergency health alert: Entire population likely affected. Severe irritation and respiratory distress. Keep windows shut.
