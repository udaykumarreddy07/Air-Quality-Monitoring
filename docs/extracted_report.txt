PROBLEM STATEMENT

Organizations and environmental agencies that monitor public health record atmospheric telemetry as sensor observation logs. A typical ambient air quality record contains a station identifier, observation timestamp, geographic coordinates, concentration levels of critical pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3), a composite Air Quality Index (AQI) score, a categorized hazard band, and corresponding health advisory instructions. When thousands of such multi-dimensional readings are stored in static spreadsheets or uncurated tabular databases, it becomes exceedingly difficult to comprehend how severe atmospheric pollution is, which toxic chemicals dominate specific airsheds, which urban centers experience acute pollution surges, and where hazardous plumes are concentrated across the country. Scanning rows one by one in spreadsheets is slow, cognitively taxing, and masks spatial and temporal patterns essential for environmental review and clinical intervention.

Two distinct dimensions of analytical understanding are urgently required. The first is an empirical understanding of severity and risk: how environmental readings are distributed across statutory Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) categories (Good, Moderate, Unhealthy for Sensitive, Unhealthy, Very Unhealthy, Hazardous), how individual chemical sub-indices correlate with the composite AQI, and how observed particulate levels compare against World Health Organization (WHO) safety thresholds. The second is geographical and physical understanding: air quality observations are tied to specific spatial coordinates, and dynamic map representations reveal regional clustering, regional pollution transport, and population exposure zones in ways that static tables cannot convey. Raw tabular dumps alone fail to support either analytical dimension effectively.

The engineering problem addressed in this project is to design, develop, and deploy AIRWATCH, an interactive, browser-based real-time air quality visualization and environmental intelligence dashboard. The platform consolidates high-level statistical indicator cards, an interactive Leaflet satellite map with 50km hazard exposure rings, a custom HTML5 Canvas particle dispersion physics simulator, dynamic D3.js temporal spline curves, multi-city comparative rankings, and a searchable, sortable, paginated record explorer. Coordinated global filters enable users to slice data by city, date timeline presets, pollutant type, and severity bands, updating every linked visualization seamlessly.

The scope of this project is focused on interactive visual analytics and empirical data communication for CSE221 – Interactive Data Visualization using D3.js. The platform visualizes an ingested, verified dataset of 1,584 daily records covering 44 major Indian urban centers extracted from structured Excel workbooks (air_quality_india_realtime.xlsx) and historical CAAQMS datastores. It serves as an authoritative course-level demonstration of client-side D3.js visualization, responsive web design, and Node.js REST database integration.

ABSTRACT

AIRWATCH is an interactive web-based air quality intelligence and environmental data visualization dashboard developed as a course-level project for CSE221 – Interactive Data Visualization using D3.js. The primary objective of the project is to demonstrate how declarative, data-driven web visualization can make multi-dimensional atmospheric sensor records significantly easier to comprehend, explore, and analyze than static government tables or offline spreadsheets. The dashboard is constructed using standard web technologies: semantic HTML5, modern CSS3 with glassmorphism aesthetics, Vanilla ES6+ JavaScript, D3.js (v7), Leaflet (v1.9.4), SheetJS (XLSX), and a lightweight Node.js Express document database engine.

The platform visualizes an ingested, verified atmospheric dataset comprising 1,584 daily observation records spanning 44 representative cities across Northern, Southern, Western, Eastern, and Central India. The user interface provides four cohesive analytical views: Overview, Geospatial, Analytics, and Incident Explorer (Verified Records). The Overview presents global filters, active dataset telemetry, and five animated KPI cards tracking National Average AQI, PM2.5, PM10, Peak Recorded AQI, and Period Trend Shift (Δ). The Geospatial view delivers an interactive Leaflet satellite map with Google Hybrid tiles, color-coded pulsating station markers, 50km radial population hazard exposure rings, and a floating real-time AQI leaderboard. The Analytics view presents D3 continuous cubic monotone spline curves, CPCB hazard safety bands, an interactive touch-inspection crosshair HUD, and ranked comparative city bar charts. The Incident Explorer supplies a searchable, sortable, and paginated table with single-click RFC-4180 CSV and JSON data export.

Across the active dataset, the dashboard reports 1,584 verified records from 44 monitoring stations, with a nationwide average AQI of 94/500 (Moderate), an average PM2.5 concentration of 31.1 µg/m³ (2.1x the WHO safe limit), an average PM10 concentration of 45.9 µg/m³, an acute peak AQI of 319 (Gurugram hotspot), and a period-over-period trend shift of +19.5% (deteriorating). Schema validation confirmed zero duplicate IDs, zero missing dates, zero out-of-bounds AQI readings, and 100% successful coordinate geocoding across all 44 municipal stations.

The project conclusively illustrates how D3.js-driven interactive visual encodings, coupled with reactive filtering, drill-down exploration, and Canvas particle physics simulation, empower researchers, civic authorities, and citizens to track atmospheric pollution dynamics with high fidelity and sub-100 millisecond responsiveness.

CHAPTER 1

INTRODUCTION

1.1 Air Quality Monitoring & Environmental Intelligence

Environmental intelligence is actionable information derived from ambient ecological surveillance that enables civic authorities, clinical researchers, and the general public to understand, mitigate, and respond to atmospheric contamination. Regulatory authorities deploy Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) incorporating optical particle counters, Beta Attenuation Monitors, chemiluminescence analyzers, and UV fluorescence spectrometers to collect continuous multi-pollutant readings. However, raw sensory data possesses practical utility only when human decision-makers can interpret it fluently, which establishes the visual presentation layer as a vital component of environmental informatics.

1.2 Atmospheric Pollution & Health Hazards

An atmospheric air pollution incident occurs when ambient concentrations of hazardous airborne particulates or toxic gases exceed statutory physiological thresholds. Fine inhalable particles with aerodynamic diameters under 2.5 microns (PM2.5) bypass the mechanical filtration of the nasal passages, penetrating deep into pulmonary alveoli and crossing directly into systemic blood circulation. This induces endothelial injury, systemic inflammatory cascades, exacerbated chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), cardiovascular stress, and elevated mortality risks. Coarse airborne dust (PM10) causes severe upper respiratory irritation, while gaseous pollutants—Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2) from vehicular combustion, Sulfur Dioxide (SO2) from thermal power plants, and secondary ground-level Ozone (O3)—generate toxic smog blankets during seasonal meteorological inversions. Because atmospheric readings are intrinsically multi-dimensional, isolated static tables are fundamentally insufficient for holistic risk assessment.

1.3 Data Visualization and Interactive Dashboards

Data visualization maps complex numerical values to visual encoding channels such as spatial position, line trajectory, glyph length, and chromatic hue, allowing human visual cognition to recognize subtle correlations and anomalies rapidly [4]. A dashboard consolidates essential metrics onto a single screen so environmental conditions can be assessed at a glance [3]. Interactivity significantly amplifies this capability: users can dynamically brush time horizons, filter specific municipal cohorts, toggle pollutant metrics, and zoom from a national overview down to granular station telemetry [1][2].

1.4 Motivation

The central motivation for AIRWATCH is to apply state-of-the-art web visualization techniques to the challenge of understanding complex atmospheric datasets. Conventional government reporting formats—often confined to static PDF bulletins or raw CSV dumps—make it cumbersome to contrast city pollution levels, identify dominant chemical hazards, or track seasonal pollution shifts. AIRWATCH synthesizes high-level KPI cards, an interactive satellite station map, continuous D3 temporal trend curves, comparative ranking bar charts, and a verified tabular records explorer into a unified, responsive interface driven by a synchronized reactive filter state. The project also provides rigorous practical mastery of D3.js (Data-Driven Documents), which represents the core visual library of the course [5].

1.5 Project Objectives

• To design and build an interactive web dashboard presenting an ingested atmospheric dataset through summary KPI cards, an interactive map, analytical D3 charts, and a verified records explorer. • To summarize multi-pollutant concentrations (AQI, PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3) against standardized CPCB hazard categories and WHO exposure limits. • To deliver geographical visualization of air quality across 44 Indian monitoring stations with satellite imagery, pulsating sensor beacons, and 50km hazard exposure rings. • To engineer an autonomous HTML5 Canvas particle physics simulator modeling real-time fluid wind dispersion and vortex turbulence. • To provide global filtering, instant keyword search, column sorting, pagination, and single-click CSV/JSON data export. • To validate dataset schema integrity and document all empirical findings.

1.6 Project Scope

AIRWATCH is an individual, course-level visual analytics project developed for CSE221 – Interactive Data Visualization using D3.js. The platform visualizes an ingested, verified dataset of 1,584 records across 44 Indian cities in a local demonstration environment. While the system incorporates an embedded Node.js JSON document database with REST APIs and PBKDF2 authentication, it is designed for visual analytics and educational demonstration, and does not serve as a certified municipal emergency broadcast system.

CHAPTER 2

LITERATURE REVIEW

2.1 Environmental Dashboard Design & Shneiderman's Mantra

Few defines an executive dashboard as a visual display of the most critical information required to achieve defined objectives, consolidated on a single screen so that it can be monitored at a glance [3]. Effective environmental dashboards avoid decorative clutter, utilizing perceptually appropriate chart encodings for meaningful comparisons. Shneiderman’s visual information-seeking mantra—"Overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand"—serves as the foundational interaction model for scientific exploratory systems [1]. Heer and Shneiderman classify interactive dynamics into data specification, view manipulation, and process recording, highlighting operations such as filtering, sorting, selecting, and navigating [2]. In AIRWATCH, users begin with the national executive overview and can immediately zoom into a city, filter by timeline presets, or inspect an individual sensor observation.

2.2 Visual Analytics and Interactive D3.js Visualization

Visual analytics unites automated data computation with interactive visual interfaces to facilitate reasoning over complex multi-dimensional datasets [7]. Munzner’s visualization design framework establishes that visual channels—spatial position, line curvature, length, and color saturation—vary significantly in perceptual accuracy [4]. D3.js (Data-Driven Documents), introduced by Bostock et al. [5][6], revolutionized web-based visualization by binding arbitrary data arrays directly to the Document Object Model (DOM) and Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG). Rather than constraining the developer to rigid charting templates, D3 provides direct algorithmic control over mathematical scales, custom axes, spline curve generators, and continuous transition interpolators [12][16].

2.3 Geographical & Geospatial Cartography in Environmental Informatics

Atmospheric pollution is inherently spatial, governed by spatial autocorrelation where adjacent geographic areas share similar dispersion characteristics. Web-mapping libraries such as Leaflet [15] enable lightweight, high-performance projection of geographic vector features, raster satellite tile layers, and radial buffer geometries. The integration of high-resolution satellite imagery (such as Google Hybrid) with thematic sensor overlays allows researchers to observe geographic proximity between industrial clusters and urban residential zones.

2.4 Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) NAQI & WHO Benchmarks

Standardizing disparate chemical concentrations into a unified Air Quality Index is essential for effective public health communication. The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) established the National Air Quality Index (NAQI) protocol [8], utilizing a segmented linear interpolation algorithm converting pollutant sub-indices into six standardized hazard brackets: Good (0–50), Satisfactory/Moderate (51–100), Moderately Polluted (101–200), Poor (201–300), Very Poor (301–400), and Severe (401–500). In parallel, the World Health Organization (WHO) published updated Global Air Quality Guidelines [9], establishing stringent 24-hour safe limits of 15 µg/m³ for PM2.5 and 45 µg/m³ for PM10. AIRWATCH co-presents both statutory CPCB classifications and WHO benchmarks to deliver comprehensive epidemiological risk context.

2.5 Atmospheric Fluid Dynamics & Canvas Particle Simulation

Atmospheric pollutant transport is governed by physical fluid flow, advection, and turbulent vortex diffusion [10]. While full three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) requires supercomputing clusters, modern web browsers executing HTML5 Canvas can simulate lightweight Lagrangian particle models in real time [13]. By modeling over 1,500 active particulate nodes subject to velocity vectors, buoyant forces, and localized wind turbulence, web applications can visually demonstrate how toxic smog disperses across airsheds.

2.6 Summary and Gap

The literature conclusively supports dashboards that combine an executive overview, dynamic filtering, geospatial mapping, and details on demand. Existing statutory environmental portals often suffer from static tables, high rendering latencies, and lack of interactive physical modeling. AIRWATCH bridges this gap by unifying automated Excel ingestion, D3.js v7 declarative rendering, Leaflet satellite cartography, and Canvas particle physics into a single, cohesive, sub-100ms web application.

4.5 Handling of Incomplete or Missing Readings

When raw sensor streams experience brief dropouts, missing pollutant readings are imputed using a 3-day sliding temporal moving average across the specific station. If an observation completely lacks both composite AQI and primary particulate metrics, it is dropped at the validation boundary, ensuring that only verified data feeds downstream D3 visualization engines.

4.6 Preparation for Visualization

Prior to visual rendering, validated records are aggregated according to active global filters. City averages, chronological day-by-day arrays, national mean scores, and period-over-period trend deltas are computed in memory and bound directly to D3 SVG elements.

6.8 Interpretation and Limitations

The empirical outputs demonstrate that AIRWATCH significantly improves exploratory data analysis over static tables. Summary KPI cards provide immediate situational awareness, the Leaflet map contextualizes spatial exposure zones, D3 temporal splines illuminate episodic pollution surges, and the verified records table supports granular inspection. The primary scope limitation is that the dataset represents a verified historical/real-time snapshot for academic course demonstration rather than a live nationwide telecommunication grid. However, the modular architecture guarantees seamless integration with live streaming MQTT/WebSocket brokers.

CHAPTER 7

CONCLUSION

AIRWATCH is an interactive web-based dashboard for visualizing, exploring, and analyzing atmospheric air quality and pollution dynamics across India. Developed for CSE221 – Interactive Data Visualization using D3.js, the project synthesizes summary KPI telemetry, an interactive Leaflet satellite map with 50km hazard exposure rings, a 1,500-node HTML5 Canvas particle dispersion physics simulator, dynamic D3 temporal spline curves, multi-city comparative rankings, and a verified tabular records explorer.

Across the active dataset of 1,584 records from 44 monitoring stations, the platform identifies a national average AQI of 94/500 (Moderate), an average PM2.5 level of 31.1 µg/m³ (2.1x WHO limit), an acute peak AQI of 319 at Gurugram, and a deteriorating period trend shift of +19.5%. The visualizations make it substantially easier to detect severity distributions, temporal seasonality, chemical proportions, and geographic clustering than raw spreadsheet tables.

The project successfully applied semantic HTML5, modern CSS3 glassmorphism, Vanilla ES6+ JavaScript, D3.js v7, Leaflet, SheetJS, and Node.js Express, providing deep practical competence in data ingestion, schema validation, declarative coordinate scaling, spline interpolation, and interaction design. The system achieves sub-100 millisecond rendering speeds and maintains complete internal consistency across all linked views. In conclusion, AIRWATCH fulfills its engineering and academic objectives, delivering an authoritative visual analytics platform for environmental research and public health surveillance.

FUTURE WORK

The following architectural enhancements represent promising future research and technical extensions for the AIRWATCH platform:

1. Live IoT Sensor Hardware Ingestion: Future versions can connect directly to low-cost LoRaWAN, ESP32, and Arduino environmental sensor nodes deployed across municipal micro-environments via high-concurrency MQTT broker protocols and WebSocket streams, transitioning from file-based snapshots to live sensor telemetry. 2. Deep Learning Predictive Forecasting (LSTM & Transformers): Incorporating Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent neural networks or temporal Transformer models trained on historical CAAQMS telemetry and meteorological vectors (temperature inversions, relative humidity, boundary layer height) to deliver automated 24-hour and 48-hour forward-looking AQI predictions. 3. Progressive Web Application (PWA) Deployment: Packaging the platform with Service Workers and Web Push API integration to enable offline historical data caching and deliver push notifications to citizens when localized particulate matter crosses hazardous thresholds. 4. Automated Environmental Compliance Reporting: Implementing server-side headless document rendering to generate formal PDF dossiers and PPTX presentations for municipal environmental audits with a single click. 5. Pollution-Aware Green Transit Routing: Enhancing the Leaflet cartography engine with Dijkstra and A* pathfinding algorithms that calculate pedestrian and cycling routes minimizing respiratory exposure to active pollution plumes.
